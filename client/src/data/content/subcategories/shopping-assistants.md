## What shopping assistants do

Shopping assistants are AI agents that sit on the buyer-facing side of activation, guiding shoppers through discovery, recommendations, and purchase. They power conversational product search, personalized recommendations, and in-session help across ecommerce sites and the newer AI-shopping surfaces where buyers are starting to browse and buy.

What sets shopping assistants apart from every other Activate subcategory is direction: the AI talks directly to the end customer rather than managing a campaign behind the scenes. Everything else in Activate operates on the marketer's side of the equation, adjusting spend, timing, or targeting. Shopping assistants are the activation surface itself, the moment where attention that a campaign already paid for either converts into a purchase or does not.

## Who uses them and why

An ecommerce brand adds a shopping assistant so visitors can describe what they want in plain language, something closer to describing a need to a store associate than typing keywords into a search box, and get curated, in-stock recommendations back. The assistant answers product questions, handles objections a shopper might otherwise abandon over, and nudges toward checkout, which lifts conversion on traffic the brand already paid to acquire.

Ecommerce and lifecycle marketers, along with merchandising teams, tend to own this tool, treating it as a conversion-rate and average-order-value lever rather than a traffic or spend lever. Because the assistant sits at the point of highest intent, a shopper already on the product page or in checkout flow, even a modest improvement in conversion often shows up clearly in revenue, which is part of why this subcategory gets budget even on teams that are cautious about new tools elsewhere.

## What to look for

- Catalog and inventory integration depth, since a recommendation that points to an out-of-stock item breaks trust immediately and undoes whatever conversion lift the assistant was supposed to create.
- Quality of conversational understanding: whether the assistant can parse a vague, natural request the way a person would, or whether it is really just keyword matching with a chat interface on top.
- Conversion and average-order-value impact that you can actually attribute, since a shopping assistant that cannot show measurable lift is hard to justify keeping.
- Where the assistant surfaces, meaning whether it lives only on your own site or also extends to third-party AI-shopping and agent surfaces where buyers are increasingly starting their search.
- Brand voice and merchandising controls, so the assistant recommends and speaks the way your team actually wants, rather than defaulting to generic responses that feel bolted on.

## How it fits into Activate

Shopping assistants are the only Activate subcategory that talks to the end customer directly instead of managing spend or messaging behind the scenes. Campaign management, AI ad platforms, and email and outreach all work to get attention and intent to your site or inbox. Shopping assistants take over from there, converting that intent into a purchase at the exact moment it exists. Teams typically add this subcategory once acquisition is already working and the next obvious lever is what happens after the click, not before it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a shopping assistant just a chatbot with better branding?** The better ones are meaningfully different, built to understand vague, natural-language product requests and connected to live inventory so recommendations are actually in stock. A generic chatbot that keyword-matches against a product feed will frustrate shoppers in a way a genuine assistant should not.

**Where does a shopping assistant fit if I already run paid ads well?** It picks up exactly where paid activation leaves off. Ads and outreach get attention to your site; the assistant's job is converting that attention once it arrives, which is a separate problem from acquisition and often the next lever worth pulling once acquisition is already performing.

**Can a shopping assistant work outside my own website?** Some extend beyond the brand's own site into third-party AI-shopping or agent surfaces where buyers are beginning to search and compare products. Coverage here varies a lot by product, so confirm where a given assistant actually surfaces before assuming it reaches buyers wherever they are shopping.
