## What AI marketing assistants do

AI marketing assistants are conversational tools that pull research, audience, and competitive context together and help you act on it: drafting briefs, summarizing data, answering ad-hoc questions, and recommending next steps. They are less a single data source than a layer on top of your stack. Where the other Plan subcategories produce inputs, an AI assistant helps you reason across them and turn analysis into a plan, often by connecting to your live ad and analytics accounts through integrations.

Many of these assistants do not stop at planning. Plugged into an ad account, an assistant that can answer "where am I wasting spend?" is often one step away from pausing that spend for you. That is why several tools here also appear under Activate: their planning and execution sides are the same product. The primary listing reflects where a tool leads, and the cross-listing reflects what else it can do.

## Who uses them and why

A solo marketer or lean growth team uses an AI assistant as a planning partner: asking it to size a segment, compare two positioning angles, or turn a pile of research into a one-page brief. Plugged into live ad accounts, it can also answer questions like where budget is leaking, without a manual export. It compresses the gap between having data and having a decision, which is where most planning time actually goes.

Larger teams use assistants to standardize the boring parts. The same prompt produces the same brief format every time, so the analysis a senior strategist would do by hand becomes something the whole team can run in minutes.

## What to look for

- What it connects to: ad platforms, analytics, and research sources through real integrations, not copy-paste.
- Whether it acts on live account data or just chats from general knowledge.
- Quality and traceability of its outputs. Can you check its reasoning and see its sources?
- Data handling, including where your account data is processed and stored.
- Workflow fit: does it produce briefs and reports your team will actually use, or one more chat window to ignore?

## How it fits into Plan

AI assistants sit on top of the other three Plan subcategories. Market research, audience intelligence, and competitive analysis each produce inputs. The assistant is where you reason across those inputs and turn them into a decision. Because many assistants also connect to live accounts, they are often the handoff point between Plan and Activate: the same tool that helped you decide can help you execute.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is an AI marketing assistant?** A conversational tool that helps you plan and analyze marketing work: summarizing research, comparing options, drafting briefs, and answering questions about your data. The stronger ones connect to your live ad and analytics accounts, so their answers reflect your actual numbers rather than general knowledge.

**Why do some assistants also appear under Activate?** Because they do both. An assistant that reads your ad account to spot wasted spend can often act on it too. We list each tool where its core strength sits and cross-list it where it also fits, so you find it whether you are planning or executing.

**Do I still need dedicated research tools if I have an assistant?** Often yes. An assistant is only as good as what it can reach. Dedicated market research, audience, and competitive tools give it deeper, fresher inputs to reason over. Think of the assistant as the layer that ties those inputs together, not a full replacement for them.
